Building History & Management Record

From factory to homes

The former Monarch Luggage Factory is a complex of four industrial structures. The Historic Districts Council’s preservation inventory dates 5 Delevan Street to 1890 and 19 Delevan Street to 1912.

According to the 2013 inspection report, conversion to commercial lofts began in 1999, and the building received a residential Certificate of Occupancy in 2005.

Early residents lived in the building under commercial leases. Some were told by ownership to install their own cooking equipment and bathing facilities. Tenants helped turn these former industrial spaces into homes.

The fight for safe, affordable homes

In 2013, tenants commissioned an inspection that documented sewage backups, water damage, fire safety hazards, and accessibility failures. The authors concluded that the building did not meet the substantial rehabilitation criteria for an exemption from rent stabilization.

Read the inspection report and summary →

More than 13 years later, nearly all of these issues persist. Ownership has allowed the building to deteriorate even further, while raising rents ever higher.

In October 2026, tenants issued a short notice to ownership that MLFTA had re-formed and requested a meeting. The letter outlines several shared concerns; it does not catalogue the many problems in individual units and throughout the building.

These include persistent water leaks, mold growth, rodent and other pest infestations, continual sewage backups, and a lack of heat, hot water, and basic building safety measures. See recent photos of building and unit conditions.
